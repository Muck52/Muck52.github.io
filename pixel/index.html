Pixel cats · study 01: the Norwegian Forest Cat

The goal is a sprite that is adorable at a glance and still unmistakably this breed to someone who knows cats. This first draft is a sitting cat in profile, 62 × 66 px, brown mackerel tabby & white, the coat most people picture for a "Wegie".

Pixel art has to work at 1×, where only the silhouette and a few colour masses survive. The tall tufted ear, the triangular head, the white bib and the big tail plume should all still read at 1×.

Breed standard → pixel decisions

Each row is a point from the breed standard (as written by FIFe and TICA; summarised from memory, so check against the current standards) and what the sprite does about it.

Where it leans adorable

  • Head about 20% larger than true proportion. A bigger head relative to the body is the strongest "cute" cue, and it also gives the breed-defining face more pixels.
  • Oversized glossy eye with a big catchlight and a slightly lifted outer corner.
  • A one-pixel smile and a whisker-pad highlight.
  • Soft, warm palette: the outline is brown, not black, and it lightens to a soft brown on lit edges, so the cat looks fluffy, not inked.
  • Rounded, pear-shaped mass, with the tail wrapped round the paws like a scarf.

What stays strictly true is everything a breed person checks first: the straight profile, the triangular head, the ear set with lynx tips, the ruff, the britches and the tail.
