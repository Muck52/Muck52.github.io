One cat, four bit depths

The same sitting Norwegian Forest Cat, kept cute and kept true to the breed, drawn at 8-bit, 16-bit, 32-bit and, for the fun of it, 64-bit. Everything the art relies on comes from the Norwegian Forest Cat breed study.

N64-era "rendered" look: shading is lit, not painted. A height field from the silhouette gives each pixel a surface normal, lit from the top-left and snapped to 4–6 step ramps, with a faint fur grain.

The same five breed markers survive at every tier, in priority order: triangle head with a straight profile, high-set tall ears, the ruff, a heavy wrapped tail, and a solid body.

Where it leans cute

  • Head about 20% larger than life: the strongest cuteness cue, and it gives the breed-defining face more pixels.
  • Oversized glossy eye with a big catchlight.
  • A one-pixel smile and a whisker-pad highlight.
  • A warm brown outline that softens on lit edges, so the fur looks fluffy rather than inked.
  • A pear-shaped mass with the tail wrapped round the paws like a scarf.

Where it stays true

  • The straight profile and triangular head.
  • High-set ears with lynx tips.
  • The ruff and the britches.
  • A tail as heavy as a leg.
  • A coat pattern the breed allows.

These are what a breed person checks first, so none of them bends.
