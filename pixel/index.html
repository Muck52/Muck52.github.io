Norwegian Forest Cat sprite set

A 16-bit-style sprite set built from the reference sheet: a majestic seated portrait and four everyday poses, in one shared 32-colour woodland palette, with the seated portrait animated hunting a mouse. The breed facts behind the design are in the Norwegian Forest Cat breed study.

Seated portrait: hunting a mouse

One continuous motion, sampled at 6, 9 and 12 frames over the same 1.8-second loop, so the only difference is smoothness. The mouse darts in to sniff and darts away again. The cat's head trails it with a slight lag and lowers to focus as it comes closer. His tail tip twitches, the far ear flicks as the mouse leaves, and the body rises one pixel with each breath. Every pixel is from the original art, moved only by whole pixels.

Design philosophy

Five principles, read from the reference sheet. Each one is carried by specific pixels in the set.

01

Breed silhouette

A sturdy, heavy-boned frame rather than a sleek one. A triangular head, large green eyes, and black lynx tufts on both ear tips that read even at 1×.

02

Layered fur

Fluff comes from pixel clusters and a jagged, broken contour rather than smooth curves. The cream ruff is the biggest mass on the cat, set against short, tight fur on the face.

03

Mackerel tabby

Dark broken stripes over a warm brown and beige ground, running consistently across the body, the legs and the very thick, ringed tail.

04

Woodland palette

Warm browns, beiges, charcoals and creams, plus soft grey-greens and pinks reserved for eyes and nose. Near-black outlines make it pop on dark grey.

05

Personality in poses

Beyond the seated portrait: a low stalk, a relaxed stand, a playful paw swipe and a deep back-arch stretch. A majestic wild cat with cosy housecat energy.

How it was made

  1. Native grid. The reference image is the art upscaled about 3.2×. Edge periodicity gives the true pitch (3.196 × 3.248 px), so the sheet is 320 × 176 art pixels. Each cell takes the median of its centre, which also strips JPEG noise.
  2. Cut-out. The flat background and the cool ground shadows are flood-filled in from the outside. The black outline stops the fill, so dark fur is never mistaken for shadow. Shadows are drawn separately in code.
  3. Palette. Median cut over the fur, plus reserved slots for eye greens and nose pinks so the small accents survive. Accents only land where the source was green or pink.
  4. Cleanup. Near-identical stray pixels join their neighbours, specks under 3 px are removed, and no holes are left inside a sprite.
  5. Animation. The hunt warps the seated sprite with a smooth displacement field and samples it pixel by pixel, so frames reuse the original art moved by whole pixels. The mouse is a small hand-drawn 3-frame sprite.
